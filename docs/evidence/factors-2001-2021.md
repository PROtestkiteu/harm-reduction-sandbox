# Harm-reduction factor evidence, slice A (2001 to 2021, plus Vogels 2009 and Boys 2001)

Source folder: `/Volumes/2TB/Data/Cloud/MEGA-Sync/MEGAsync/Chemical Safety/Wiedza/HR Drug Checking/` (read-only). Plus `Wiedza/Understanding reasons for drug use amongst young people A functional perspective.pdf` (Boys, Marsden, Strang 2001).

How this was produced: text of all 82 PDFs in the slice was extracted with macOS PDFKit (no source file touched), then each paper was read at abstract, introduction, results and discussion level (long reports: summary, conclusions and keyword hits). No .docx files fell in the slice. Page numbers are PDF page indices (the Nth page of the PDF file), which may differ from printed journal page numbers. Numbers are as reported in each source; "cited" means the paper quotes another study's figure. Evidence types: SR = systematic review, RCT, cohort, XS = cross-sectional survey, qual = qualitative, lab, guideline, opinion.

Outcome vocabulary used for the simulation: **demand** (people needing or seeking the service), **attendance** (people actually reaching it), **unmet need**, **adverse outcomes**, plus **capacity / staffing**. Direction is written as up / down (or + / -).

Papers read: 81 files with usable text, out of 82 in the slice (one file holds only repository cover pages). Several files are versions of one study (Jacka 2020 x2, one RCT protocol; Beaulieu 2020 and 2021; Lockwood 2020 and 2021; the three 2001 EMCDDA files are one inventory), so about 76 distinct studies.

Close runners-up: police agreement or tolerance zone with the service (11 papers, mostly descriptive, no effect size); siting near the open drug scene (Beaulieu 2021: stimulant pre-use checking OR 2.33 outside the Vancouver DTES vs 1.33 inside; Long 2020: living in DTES AOR 1.68 for using checking); sellers using checking as quality control (14 papers; Larnder 2021: 12% of 1,653 checks were to sell); peer staff (12 papers, qualitative only).

Main caveats across the slice: most attendance figures are stated intentions from self-selected surveys (Barratt 2017, Southey 2020); behaviour-change data are self-reported at point of service; outcome data on adverse events are almost all uncontrolled before and after comparisons (Measham 2019 hospital admissions 19 of 59 in 2015 vs 1 of 50 in 2016); settings cluster in Australia, Canada (Vancouver, Victoria BC), UK and Western Europe.

# Paper-level caveats and notes

- 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Summary.pdf: Caveats: no hard outcome data; 5 of 8 projects evaluated some questions, 2 claim full evaluation; also no evidence that testing promotes use or helps dealers. p7, p8, p9.
- 2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group with high consumption frequency and polydrug use.pdf: Caveats: self-selected users, questionnaire items changed over time (12 month to 30 day recall from 2008). p5.
- 2012_Winstock_The prevalence and natural history of urinary symptoms among recreational ketamine users.pdf: Caveats: self-selected, self-report, no validated dependence measure. p3.
- 2013_Chiauzzi_Participatory Research With an Online Drug Forum A Survey of User Characteristics, Information Sharing, and Ha.pdf: Caveats: self-selected, 38% survey abandonment, mainly male (75.8%). p10.
- 2015_EU_NEWIP_Drug checking_standards.pdf: Evaluation indicator: number of persons who do not consume after being informed. p69.
- 2015_Maddox_Constructive activism in the dark web cryptomarkets and illicit drugs in the digital ‘demimonde’.pdf: Caveats: 17 participants, 15 male, not representative, claims unverifiable. p11, p13.
- 2016_Barratt_Safer scoring Cryptomarkets, social supply and drug market violence.pdf: Caveats: self-selected; 5.5% of 97,855 GDS respondents had ever used cryptomarkets. p8.
- 2018_Karamouzian_Evaluation of a fentanyl drug checking service for clients of a supervised injection facility, Vancouver, Cana.pdf: adverse outcomes | overdose with positive check: service data | 120 overdoses associated with checks, 94% post-consumption testers; positive vs negative OR 5.97 (2.41-14.78); 76.2% (n=92) needed naloxone, OR 4.42 (1.77-11.02); since 2003 over 3.6 million visits and over 6000 overdoses treated, none fatal | reverse causality: checking may follow an overdose | p3-p5
