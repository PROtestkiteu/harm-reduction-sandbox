# Harm Reduction Simulator: factor evidence, slice B (2022 to 2026 papers)

Source folder: `/Volumes/2TB/Data/Cloud/MEGA-Sync/MEGAsync/Chemical Safety/Wiedza/HR Drug Checking/` (read only, nothing modified).

Slice: 135 PDF files (every file starting 2022 to 2026, plus `Cannabis-adulterated-with-the-synthetic-cannabinoid-rec_2022_International-J.pdf`). Text was extracted page by page with macOS PDFKit into the scratchpad (`scratchpad/txt/`). Page numbers below are PDF page indices of that extraction (page 1 = first PDF page, which for some preprints and repository copies is a cover page). "p1 abstract" means the figure appears in the abstract.

Outcome shorthand used in every factor:
- DEM = demand (people who need or seek the service)
- ATT = attendance (people actually reaching it)
- UNMET = unmet need
- ADV = adverse outcomes (overdose, poisoning, hospitalisation, unintended exposure)
- CAP = service capacity / staffing / sustainability
- Arrows: up = increases, down = decreases, (?) = direction plausible but not measured

Evidence type shorthand: SR = systematic review, ScR = scoping review, RCT, cohort, XS = cross-sectional survey, QUAL = qualitative, MIXED = mixed methods, LAB = lab study, EVAL = programme/implementation evaluation, GUIDE = expert guideline / consensus, OP = opinion / commentary / letter, MON = drug-market monitoring data.

General caveats that apply to almost everything below: most studies are cross-sectional or qualitative, from North America, Australia or Western Europe, with convenience samples; behavioural outcomes are mostly stated intentions, not observed behaviour; very few studies measure overdose or mortality directly. Maghsoudi 2022 (SR, 90 studies, 87.7% cross-sectional) states this explicitly.

## 1. national_law

### 1.1 Criminalisation of drug possession (prohibition as structural stigma)
- Moves: ATT down, UNMET up, DEM roughly unchanged or up (need exists but is suppressed); ADV up indirectly (unregulated supply).
- Davis 2022 (QUAL, secondary analysis of 26 interviews with potential service users, Victoria BC, Canada): drug checking operates in a context of structural stigma produced by criminalisation; people fear criminal repercussions, anticipate stigma and avoid services; authors suggest decriminalisation could address root causes. `2022_Davis_Substance Use Stigma and Community Drug Checking...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Wallace 2022a "Everywhere and for everyone" (QUAL, 26 interviews, BC): criminalisation and stigma, geography and cultural inappropriateness deter use by a broad population. `2022_Wallace_Everywhere and for everyone...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Wallace 2022b market intervention (QUAL, 26 interviews incl. people who sell drugs, Victoria BC, CFIR): criminalisation and market pressures named as the most critical implementation factors; services need locations "without the risk of arrest". `2022_Wallace_Implementing drug checking as an illicit drug market intervention...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Moran 2024 (QUAL, providers via Zoom Jun to Nov 2022 plus clients in San Francisco Nov 2022): one provider frames prohibition and law-enforcement harassment as root causes that create a bad supply and prevent people investigating it; criminalisation regulates access to health care, housing, employment. `2024_Moran_How do we understand the value of drug checking...pdf` p3, p6, p10.
- Ondocsin 2023 (QUAL, 11 provider interviews, US/Canada, 2022): providers operate under threat of prosecution for themselves and clients; legal exposure pushed some programmes to mobile models. `2023_Ondocsin_Insights from Drug Checking Programs Practicing Bootstrap Public Health...pdf` p7.
- Grace 2023b scoping review (ScR, 29 articles): legality and privacy are two of four primary implementation themes and reduce utilisation. `2023_Grace_Contextual factors affecting the implementation of drug checking...pdf` p1 abstract, p3.
- Grace 2023a (QUAL, 7 interviews, 10 participants, Illinois): criminalisation and loss of privacy were major barriers; staff fear felony charges for mailing samples to labs. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment of key considerations...pdf` p1 abstract, p4 to p5.
- Puljevic 2025 (QUAL, 57 interviews, Queensland, Australia): fear of being "punished or apprehended" listed among barriers alongside cost and wait time. `2025_Puljević_'It'd Be Nice to Know...'.pdf` p5.
- Falzon 2023 (QUAL, 43 interviews, 3 Scottish cities, pre-implementation): criminalisation and the risk environment mediate whether information changes behaviour (abstract only available). `2023_Falzon_It would really support the wider harm reduction agenda...pdf` p2.
- Barratt 2024 (MON, 103 cryptomarket samples tested by Get Your Drugs Tested, Sep 2022 to Aug 2023, Australia): under prohibition only 65% contained only the advertised substance, 14% advertised plus other substances, 21% advertised substance absent; nitazene detected. `2024_Barratt_Adulteration and substitution of drugs purchased in Australia from cryptomarkets...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Uncertainty: no study compares jurisdictions with and without criminalisation quantitatively; evidence is mostly perceived barriers.

### 1.2 Legal status / legal sanction of drug checking services themselves (exemptions, licences, government trials)
- Moves: CAP up (services can exist and scale), ATT up (reduced client fear), UNMET down.
- Caldicott 2023 (case study / OP using a change model, ACT, Australia): about 24 months of advocacy, coalition building and leadership produced Australia's first legally sanctioned festival pill testing, operational 29 April 2018; a progressive small jurisdiction helped. `2023_Caldicott_A step change model analysis...pdf` p1 abstract, p3 to p4.
- Mullin 2023 (LAB plus service pilot, UK): first UK Home Office licensed pharmacist-led community DCS embedded in a substance misuse service; small sample (n = 13). `2023_Mullin_Handheld Raman Spectroscopy in the First UK Home Office Licensed...pdf` p1.
- Bird 2026 (XS survey of 38 DCS, Latin America n = 10 vs US n = 28, plus focus group): DCS equipment reported legal in 44% of LA programmes vs 75% of US; LA staff cite legal ambiguity and lack of government support. `2026_Bird_Drug Checking Services in Latin America vs. the United States...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Kaskela 2025 (EVAL, Finland, 503 paraphernalia samples, Apr 2022 to Jul 2024, 9 services): "for legal reasons" only used paraphernalia, not drugs, could be tested; the forced lab model led to monthly analysis and low result return (see 8.2). `2025_Kaskela_From trash to results...pdf` p1 abstract, p3.
- Carroll 2022 (QUAL/observation, Boston SSP, 2019 to 2020, CFIR): state policies do not clarify legality of steps in drug checking. `2022_Carroll_The Bronze Age of drug checking...pdf` p4.
- Coulaud 2024 (QUAL, 7 interviews, Vancouver): overdose crisis created favourable legal framework and buy-in from health authorities and police; drug checking "legally has not been a possibility" before. `2024_Coulaud_Implementation opportunities and challenges...pdf` p5, p9.
- Moon 2024 (GUIDE, interdisciplinary consensus, US SSPs): "attend to legal concerns" is one of four principles for choosing a method; returning the sample (FTIR non-destructive) depends on legality. `2024_Moon_Enhancing drug checking services for supply monitoring...pdf` p1 abstract, p5.
- Reed 2024 (QUAL, 40 PWUD, Philadelphia, Dec 2021): participants were generally not concerned about unclear legal status of the service itself, but were concerned about police involvement (see 2.1). `2024_Reed_Recommendations from people who use drugs in Philadelphia...pdf` p7.
- Uncertainty: legal status mainly acts on providers (CAP) and only indirectly on clients.

### 1.3 Paraphernalia laws covering fentanyl test strips (FTS)
- Moves: CAP down (distribution illegal), ATT down, UNMET up; decriminalising FTS moves these the other way.
- Murphy 2025 (QUAL, 13 police chiefs, one Pennsylvania county, FTS decriminalised in PA early 2023): all but two chiefs supported FTS decriminalisation; one chief had charged someone with possession of FTS as paraphernalia; in about half of interviews the chief did not know what FTS were. `2025_Murphy_Police Chiefs' Perspectives on Fentanyl Test Strips.pdf` p2, p4 to p5.
- Morris 2026 (QUAL content analysis, N = 29 documents 2021 to 2025, Texas): harm-reduction framing dominant and bipartisan, but FTS remain criminalised because of institutional friction in the Texas Senate ("pre-punctuational phase"). `2026_Morris_Toward policy punctuation...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Larson 2025 (EVAL): since April 2021 federal funds can buy FTS, but some states still treat FTS as paraphernalia, making distribution illegal. `2025_Larson_Implementation of a Police-Led Fentanyl Test Strip Distribution Program...pdf` p2 to p3.
- Marley 2022/2023 (XS, 592 North Carolina community pharmacists, Mar to May 2022): 79.3% somewhat/very willing to sell FTS, 86.9% to refer, 62.9% to advertise; barriers: not knowing where to order (49.8%), discomfort starting the conversation (44.9%); 88.3% wanted training. `2022_Marley_North Carolina Pharmacists' Willingness...pdf` p1 abstract (same data as `2023_Marley_...pdf`).

### 1.4 Decriminalisation of personal possession (BC 2023 framework)
- Moves: enforcement contact down; possible effect on supply composition (ADV) uncertain.
- Singh 2025 (MON, descriptive regression, Vancouver, 1 Feb to 31 Jul 2023): police recorded 1805 seizures, about 42.7% opioids (n = 771); 9085 drug checking samples; early association (p = 0.03) between monthly opioid seizures and the share of samples positive for both fentanyl and benzodiazepines (1-month lag). `2025_Singh_Probing trends of opioid seizures...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Caveat: 6 months, ecological, no causal inference; authors use it to argue for removing police from managing possession.

### 1.5 Legal status of cannabis and low-THC products
- Moves: DEM up for cannabis checking where cannabis is illegal; ADV up where legal low-THC products act as carriers for synthetic cannabinoids.
- Bastien 2025 (XS online, n = 553 people who use cannabis, France where cannabis is illegal): 73.4% willing to use a cannabis checking service; women less willing (aOR 0.54, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.86); tertiary education (aOR 2.14, 1.45 to 3.17) and therapeutic use (sometimes/often aOR 1.71, 1.09 to 2.69; always aOR 2.61, 1.25 to 5.49) more willing. `2025_Bastien_Willingness to use a drug checking service for cannabis...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Monti 2022 (MON plus self-report, Switzerland where low-THC <1% products except hashish are legal, Jan 2020 to Jul 2021): of 94 samples, 50% contained up to three synthetic cannabinoids (mostly MDMB-4en-PINACA); SC group had more adverse events (p = 0.041), psychological (p = 0.0007) and cardiological (p = 0.020). `2022_Monti_Adulteration of low-delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol products...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Cannabis-adulterated 2022 (MON, letter, 1142 suspicious cannabis samples, 9 European DCS, Apr 2020 to Apr 2021): 270 (23.6%) contained MDMB-4en-PINACA; all adulterated samples low THC (<1%); three serious incidents needing hospitalisation; also found in cannabis sold in Dutch coffeeshops. `Cannabis-adulterated-with-the-synthetic-cannabinoid-rec_2022_International-J.pdf` p1 to p2.
- Caveat: self-selected "suspicious" samples; not market prevalence.

### 1.6 Legal status of image and performance enhancing drugs (AAS)
- Moves: ADV up (counterfeit/substandard products), ATT to health care down (stigma), DEM for specialised checking up.
- Piatkowski 2024 (QUAL, 6 women within 15 AAS users, Australia where AAS are criminalised): women report harder access to reliable suppliers, female-specific compounds commonly faked. `2024_Piatkowski_"The compounds for females are really commonly faked!"...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Magnolini 2025 (EVAL pilot, Zurich DCS from Aug 2023, 52 clients, 71 samples, GC-MS): 52% of samples fake (25% counterfeit, 27% substandard); anonymous and free; NPS 97, CSAT 93% (N = 30 answered). `2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids...pdf` p1 abstract, p3 to p4, p7.
- Akrigg 2026 (XS, Global Drug Survey 2024, 1366 men, 134 women using IPEDs): GP access difficulty men 17.2% vs women 3.0%; men more likely to use lab testing for purity (13.0% vs 3.7%, p = .002); home tests 7.0% vs 3.0% (ns). Background: stigma and fear of judgement deter disclosure to GPs; women face "double" stigma. `2026_Akrigg_Gender Differences in Health Services Access...pdf` p1 abstract, p4 to p5.
- Piatkowski 2025b (QUAL, 25 Australian AAS consumers, 40 interactions): lack of evidence-based injecting guidance; knowledge from forums and peers; infections and abscesses reported. `2025_Piatkowski_Creating Safer Injecting Practices...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 1.7 National regulatory shocks and market regulation (Brexit, lockdowns, scheduling)
- Moves: ADV up through unexpected substitution in recreational markets.
- MJ 2022 "Cathinone Hydra" (MON, 1648 samples at 3 English festivals 2019 and 2021, plus survey of 1124 attendees): MDMA detection in MDMA-like samples fell from 93% (2019, n = 417) to 55% (2021, n = 377); cathinones and caffeine each about one fifth of 2021 samples; 35%+ intended MDMA use but 1% or less reported cathinone/caffeine use (unintentional consumption). `2022_MJ_The Cathinone Hydra...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Measham 2024 (XS, English Festival Study 2014 to 2023, n = 11,566): poppers use concentrated in gay and bisexual men; context of ambiguous legal status and a 2024 ACMD recommendation for exemption from the Psychoactive Substances Act. `2024_Measham_Poppers, the Politics of Exemption...pdf` p1.

## 2. enforcement_policing

### 2.1 Police presence near the service / fear of arrest at or around the site
- Moves: ATT down strongly, UNMET up; DEM unchanged.
- Reed 2024 (QUAL, 40 PWUD, Philadelphia): participants said police involvement in any form (running or collecting information) would lead them not to participate; some feared the site would become a hotspot for mass arrests. `2024_Reed_Recommendations...pdf` p7.
- Summers 2026 (MIXED, 106 harm reduction housing residents surveyed Sep 2023 to Feb 2024, 28 interviewed, 55 samples in 9 on-site sessions from May 2024, Boston): after the November 2023 mass encampment sweep and increased downtown policing, residents preferred on-site checking over off-site SSPs "given the heavy police presence" there; 44.3% had already used SSP drug checking; 49% "very likely" and 25% "somewhat likely" to use on-site checking. `2026_Summers_Real time, on-site drug checking in low-threshold housing communities.pdf` p3 to p4, p8 to p9.
- Moran 2024 (QUAL, San Francisco clients): fear that bystanders would film and call police, or of being an "easy target" for harassment, when using mobile or site-based spectroscopy. `2024_Moran...pdf` p5.
- Carroll 2022 (QUAL, Boston): aggressive law enforcement puts staff and participants at risk; staff with lived experience may have criminal records and be more exposed. `2022_Carroll...pdf` p4.
- Reed 2022 (QUAL, 29 PWUD with FTS experience, Philadelphia, Jan to May 2021): whether people test depends on micro-context including likelihood of encountering police and testing location. `2022_Reed_You cant go wrong being safe...pdf` p9.
- Grace 2023a (QUAL, Illinois): fear of surveillance and criminalisation are major barriers. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment...pdf` p4.
- Larson 2025 (background): historical over-policing has led PWUD, particularly people of colour, to fear and avoid police. `2025_Larson...pdf` p3.

### 2.2 Police approach to the service (agreements, MOUs, non-interference)
- Moves: ATT up, CAP up (safer operation).
- Pocuca 2025 (QUAL, 23 AOD staff, Australia, pre-implementation): agreements or memoranda of understanding protecting clients from over-policing named as one of the paramount uptake factors with confidentiality. `2025_Pocuca_Understanding Drug and Alcohol Staff Perspectives...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Falzon 2023 (QUAL, 43 interviews, Scotland): approach of local and national police and public/community attitudes described as key external factors for success. `2023_Falzon_Planning and implementing community-based drug checking services in Scotland...pdf` p2.
- Coulaud 2024: police departments among organisations mobilised by the overdose crisis to support the pilot. `2024_Coulaud...pdf` p9.

### 2.3 Police confiscation of drug checking supplies
- Moves: CAP down, ATT down.
- Bird 2026 (XS, 38 programmes): law enforcement confiscating DCS supplies reported by 38% of LA programmes vs 11% of US; cartel violence also cited in LA. `2026_Bird...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 2.4 Police-led FTS distribution (police as distributors)
- Moves: DEM up modestly, ATT to referrals up; but trust caveat (PWUD fear police).
- Larson 2022 / 2025 (EVAL mixed methods, One2One, 21 police departments, Massachusetts and Maine, Jan to Nov 2021): 2556 FTS kits (3 strips each) and 3703 referrals or direct services; engagement ratio 1.44 referrals/services per kit; kit distribution impact rate 4.37 kits per fatal overdose rate (per 100,000); site engagement ratio range 0.08 to 4.15, correlation with impact rate r = 0.38; street outreach and post-overdose outreach accounted for 36% and 18% of kits. Higher uptake where naloxone co-distributed, varied strategies, pre-existing community partnerships, leadership champions, prior harm reduction awareness and prioritised training. Authors call it a "modest complement" to community-led distribution. `2022_Larson_Police-led Fentanyl Test Strip Distribution Program...pdf` p2, p4 to p5; `2025_Larson...pdf` p2, p4 to p5.
- Caveat: no outcome on overdose; recipients' trust in police not quantified; self-reported administrative data.

### 2.5 Police knowledge and attitudes toward harm reduction
- Moves: through policy and street-level discretion, ATT and CAP.
- Murphy 2025 (QUAL, 13 chiefs): hesitant support; fear of a "slippery slope" toward SSPs they oppose; scepticism that PWUD change behaviour; concern that FTS could be used to seek fentanyl and increase overdoses; author recommends police training. `2025_Murphy...pdf` p2, p4 to p5.
- Larson 2025: departments with prior harm reduction awareness and prioritised trainings performed better. `2025_Larson...pdf` p2.

### 2.6 Police seizures vs community drug checking as early warning
- Moves: ADV down when community checking is the warning source (earlier detection).
- Silcox 2026 (cohort/MON preprint, Massachusetts Drug Supply Data Stream, 2430 samples Apr 2018 to Dec 2025, 8 locations; 68.1% community partners, 31.9% police departments): emergent substances (xylazine, medetomidine, bromazolam, nitazenes) detected a median of 249 days earlier (IQR 52.5 to 732) in community than co-located police samples (199 days if tested at acquisition); police samples more often fentanyl primary (74.2% vs 64%), less often xylazine (15.0% vs 27.4%); associated fatal overdose 62.9% (PD) vs 10.9% (CP). `2026_Silcox_Source Matters...pdf` p1 abstract, p7.
- Caveat: preprint, not peer reviewed; submitter data missing for most samples.

### 2.7 Supply disruption by enforcement (encampment sweeps, seizures)
- Moves: ADV up (destabilised supply), DEM up.
- Summers 2026: sweeps and increased policing destabilised local supply and pushed residents away from SSPs; 72.7% of 55 on-site samples had both expected substance and an unsuspected contaminant; xylazine suspected in 9.1% but present in 32.7%. `2026_Summers...pdf` p3, p5, p9.
- Singh 2025 (see 1.4).

### 2.8 Police access to service data / identifiability
- Moves: ATT down if data could be used by police; CAP (data governance burden).
- Moon 2024 (GUIDE): published individual-level results must protect anonymity to avoid criminalisation and retaliation from sellers for perceived "snitching"; scrutinise what data could be used by police. `2024_Moon...pdf` p5, p7.
- Liddle 2025 (EVAL of ToxiBot, Argentina, 2549 records to Jul 2026): paper records kept by outreach teams increased exposure to police intervention, motivating a digital tool; legal and data-governance risks redistributed. `2025_Liddle_ToxiBot (Eleusis 3).pdf` p1 to p2.

## 3. health_system_funding

### 3.1 Stable public funding and national integration of drug checking
- Moves: CAP up, ATT up, UNMET down; reach to hidden populations up.
- Verdenik 2026 (EVAL long term, Slovenia 2018 to 2024): service moved from NGO initiatives to a nationally funded, policy-integrated measure (ESF project from late 2018, then state project funding) linked to the national early warning system; anonymous, free, results in 4 to 7 days; 4338 visits, about 2759 estimated unique users, 5518 samples; 82.9% of users had no prior contact with any drug service; about 24 public warnings per year; 73.7% received new information at submission (n = 1523); 23% intended not to use after an unexpected result; 81% men. `2026_Verdenik_'I want to know what I'm taking'...pdf` p1 abstract, p3 to p5, p7, p10.
- Bird 2026: funding gaps a major barrier for 80% of LA vs 54% of US programmes. `2026_Bird...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Carroll 2022: restricted grant funding; spectrometers expensive and confirmatory testing an ongoing large expense; strong funder relationships a facilitator. `2022_Carroll...pdf` p1 abstract, p4 to p5.
- Ondocsin 2023: programmes run "bootstrap public health" in resource-constrained conditions. `2023_Ondocsin...pdf` p4.
- Moon 2024: legal complexity plus cost and labour burdens on already stretched SSPs. `2024_Moon...pdf` p3.
- Moran 2024: overdose crisis attracted new funding and programming for harm reduction in California. `2024_Moran...pdf` p1 to p2.
- Pires 2024 / Vale 2025 (QUAL, Porto vs Lisbon): pandemic adaptations sit within an overall process of disinvestment in harm reduction in Portugal. `2024_Pires_City-level drug policies in Portugal...pdf` p1 abstract (same study as `2025_Vale_...pdf`).

### 3.2 Integration of drug checking into existing health and harm reduction services
- Moves: ATT up for drug checking and for other services; DEM met at lower threshold.
- Berry 2025 (EVAL, hierarchical linear model, Maryland SSPs with Rapid Analysis of Drugs mail-in lab checking): RAD affiliation associated with +74% participant encounters per quarter and +131% wound care services; counselling changed little; associations held after adjusting for SSP maturity. `2025_Berry_Impact of Lab-Based Drug Checking Services on Syringe Service Program Engagement...pdf` p1 to p2 abstract.
- Kannam 2024 (cohort, 172 ED visits offered harm reduction kits, Chicago, Aug 2022 to Aug 2023): 85.8% of 113 with pharmacist notes accepted FTS; 86.1% of 94 had never used FTS; 62.1% of 66 said they would not use or would dispose of a positive substance, 10.6% use less, 25.7% unsure; follow-up reached only 24 of 110 (21.8%), of whom 50% had not yet used strips. `2024_Kannam_Fentanyl test strip distribution acceptability and use among substance-related emergency department visits.pdf` p1 to p2.
- Rife-Pennington 2026 (cohort QI, 57 male veterans in VA SUD clinics, 2020): 98.2% accepted at least one FTS kit, 43.9% naloxone; knowledge gains d = 0.74 to 1.21; 22.2% reported using FTS at one month. `2026_Rife-Pennington_Pharmacist-Led Fentanyl Test Strip Education...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Sutherland 2025 (cohort, 78 people who used heroin, Sydney 2020 to 2021): 93% would like a drug checking service, preferably via a supervised injecting facility or NSP. `2025_Sutherland_Characteristics of take-home fentanyl test strip use...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Gomez 2025 (MON, NYC health department with SSP partners, Nov 2021 to Dec 2024): municipal health department ran checking as a public health programme (see 7.1 for results). `2025_Gomez_Community-based drug checking at syringe service programs in New York City...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Wallace 2022a: integrate drug checking within existing health care services to extend reach. `2022_Wallace_Everywhere...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 3.3 Mass media plus free product distribution (state level)
- Moves: DEM up, ATT (uptake) up.
- Kirk 2025 (EVAL, RE-AIM, New York State, 22 months of operational data from 2022): test strip demand rose with each campaign release; all counties adopted by end of period; county order volume only weakly correlated with overdose death rate (r = 0.0014, p = .05), and most populous counties not always highest demand. `2025_Kirk_Using RE-AIM to evaluate...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Caveat: orders, not use; correlation reported as tiny.

### 3.4 Pharmacy channel and professional training
- Moves: CAP up, DEM met.
- Marley 2022/2023 (see 1.3). Marley 2025 (co-design, NC): no training existed for community pharmacists on FTS/XTS; co-developed one with advisory board. `2025_Marley_Co-development of a community pharmacy training...pdf` p1.

### 3.5 Lab and technical capacity (confirmatory testing, technician training, software)
- Moves: CAP up, quality/trust up, turnaround down.
- Estrada 2026 (EVAL, NYC SSPs, 362 samples): technicians reached the 90% concordance target with secondary lab (90.7% and 92.9%) after 33 and 38 weeks of training. `2026_Estrada_Advancing Spectroscopy-Based Drug Checking Training...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Coulaud 2024: new technicians get a two-day intensive training plus at least 30 hours shadowing. `2024_Coulaud...pdf` p7.
- Gozdzialski 2025 (EVAL software, Vancouver Island, May 2022 to Dec 2024): kiosk/remote software enabled 6 remote sites, 2673 samples for 1926 clients, including hundreds first-time users. `2025_Gozdzialski_A software framework for infrared spectral analysis...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Grace 2023a: FTIR preferred (accuracy, range, portability, usability); confirmatory testing trains technicians and covers limits. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment...pdf` p1 abstract, p5.
- Thompson 2023 (GUIDE, Toronto DCS): little regulatory oversight of technologies; list of questions for purchasing. `2023_Thompson_Considerations for Purchasing Drug Checking Technologies...pdf` p1.

## 4. local_government_planning

### 4.1 City council stance (zero tolerance vs harm reduction)
- Moves: ATT up and service adherence up under harm-reduction-oriented councils; UNMET up under restrictive ones.
- Pires 2024 / Vale 2025 (QUAL comparative, 22 street-involved PWUD and 12 professionals, Porto and Lisbon, COVID-19): Porto described as restrictive and zero-tolerance, Lisbon as harm-reduction focused with better responsiveness and better adherence of street-involved PWUD, mostly because of inclusive approaches; city-level policy diverged despite one national model (Portugal decriminalisation). `2024_Pires...pdf` p1 abstract; `2025_Vale...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 4.2 Local political support (councils, mayors) in smaller communities
- Moves: CAP (permission, siting) and ATT.
- Hutchison 2025 (QUAL, 39 interviews, six services in four smaller communities on Vancouver Island, BC): responses expected to range from welcoming to oppositional; resistance reflects local politics, town councils and mayors; lack of local political support named as a barrier; implementers say they are used to navigating opposition; some communities expected to welcome it after heavy overdose losses (including one rural Indigenous community). `2025_Hutchison_Implementing community drug checking in smaller urban communities...pdf` p1 abstract, p5.

### 4.3 Geographic availability (rural and small towns, distance)
- Moves: UNMET up, ATT down where no local service.
- Karden 2025 (XS, European Web Survey on Drugs Austria data, n = 1113, Mar to May 2021): 20.1% had ever used DCS; reasons for non-use: trust in source of supply 68%, confidence in quality 64%, lack of service near residence 62%; missing availability reported by 82.6% of rural residents; at the time DCS existed only in Vienna and Innsbruck; those living with parents, in rural/small towns, and cannabis-only users used less. `2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria...pdf` p1 abstract, p6 to p7.
- Fadanelli 2026 (QUAL, 29 PWUD, six rural Appalachian Kentucky counties): only 7 of 29 had heard of FTS beforehand, and 1 had a current source; unreliable EMS response in remote areas made FTS especially valued; 10 of 11 with a positive result changed behaviour; one third passed FTS through networks. `2026_Fadanelli_"I wasn't scared of the dope I had anymore..."...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Hohmann 2026 (XS, n = 206 US adults via MTurk): urban residents reported higher comfort using/accessing FTS than rural (3.61 vs 3.29 on 5-point scale, p = 0.048). `2026_Hohmann_Public Knowledge and Perceptions of Fentanyl Test Strips...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Hutchison 2025: geographic profile and resource availability are two of four contextual factors in small communities. `2025_Hutchison...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Wallace 2022a: services should be located widely throughout communities (proportionate universalism). `2022_Wallace_Everywhere...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Puljevic 2025: travel to a separate location is a deterrent. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p6.
- Tilhou 2022 (MIXED, 341 surveyed, 29 interviewed, Wisconsin SSP clients): regular transportation and diverse distribution locations support test strip use. `2022_Tilhou_Characteristics and context of fentanyl test strip use...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 4.4 Housing policy (harm reduction housing) as a service setting
- Moves: ATT up when checking is on site.
- Summers 2026 (see 2.1, 2.7): on-site checking at three Boston harm reduction housing sites was preferred and convenient. `2026_Summers...pdf` p4, p8.

### 4.5 Event licensing and festival permission
- Moves: CAP and ATT at events.
- Piercey 2025 (XS, 227 attendees, 4-day Colorado festival): most US festivals do not permit on-site drug checking; barriers to self-checking included cost and sourcing, weather, festival restrictions, lack of a secure testing location, legal concerns, social dynamics and lack of training; authors recommend allowing attendees to bring supplies and providing protected testing zones. `2025_Piercey_Use of reagent test kits and fentanyl test strips among electronic music festival attendees...pdf` p1 abstract, discussion.
- Caldicott 2023 (see 1.2).

## 5. community_attitudes_stigma

### 5.1 General public support for drug checking
- Moves: enabling condition for policy (CAP), weak indirect effect on ATT.
- Caluzzi 2023 (XS, 2019 National Drug Strategy Household Survey, Australia): 55.8% supported drug checking; higher among 25 to 34 years (62%), most advantaged (66%), income over AUD 104,000 (64%), bachelor degree or higher (65%), major cities (58%), recent users of commonly tested drugs (88%) and other drugs (76.5%), risky drinkers (64%); in multivariable models younger people, women and the most educated were more supportive than 55+, men and less educated. `2023_Caluzzi_Who supports drug-checking services in Australia...pdf` p1 abstract, p4 to p5.
- Morris 2026: bipartisan harm-reduction framing in Texas has not overcome institutional friction (see 1.3).

### 5.2 Neighbourhood or community opposition to a harm reduction service
- Moves: CAP down (siting, political cover), ATT down (services hide), UNMET up.
- Moran 2024 (QUAL): harm reduction is often criticised as "enabling" and making neighbourhoods less safe; community pushback leads legislators relying on constituents to shy away despite evidence. `2024_Moran...pdf` p3, p11.
- Ondocsin 2023 (QUAL): growing hostility to harm reduction in parts of California; one programme deliberately avoided drawing attention to the service. `2023_Ondocsin...pdf` p7.
- Hutchison 2025 (QUAL, small BC communities): it is "hard for people not to be judgmental"; expected reactions from welcoming to oppositional. `2025_Hutchison...pdf` p5.
- Falzon 2023 (QUAL, Scotland): attitudes of the public and local community could influence success; dialogue between stakeholders and tailored communications suggested to build support. `2023_Falzon_Planning...pdf` p2.
- Murphy 2025: police chiefs' fear of a slippery slope toward syringe services mirrors conservative community concern. `2025_Murphy...pdf` p2.
- Caveat: no study measures attendance before and after a specific opposition episode.

### 5.3 Visibility of the site and fear of being recognised (close-knit communities)
- Moves: ATT down, particularly in small towns and for occasional users.
- Hutchison 2025: sites widely recognised as harm reduction points; reluctance to be seen; fear of different treatment by local business owners; close kin networks mean clients avoid talking to staff they know. `2025_Hutchison...pdf` p6.
- Davis 2022: participants recommended public yet private locations preserving anonymity; perceived hierarchy of substances leads to avoidance of sites associated with certain drugs. `2022_Davis...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Pocuca 2025: co-location makes it ambiguous which service someone is visiting (facilitator), but NSP co-location may deter infrequent users because of injecting stigma. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p5.

### 5.4 Self-identity and internalised stigma ("I am not a drug user")
- Moves: DEM expressed down among occasional users, ATT down.
- Coulaud 2024 (QUAL, SGM men pilot, Vancouver): casual users have a hard time seeing themselves as drug users, a "moral barrier" to access; stigma also burdens staff. `2024_Coulaud...pdf` p5, p9.
- Davis 2022: internalised stigma causes shame and avoidance. `2022_Davis...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Akrigg 2026, Piatkowski 2024: women using IPEDs face double stigma. `2026_Akrigg...pdf` p4 to p5.

### 5.5 Peer norms and subjective norms
- Moves: DEM up when peers endorse.
- Piatkowski 2025a (XS path analysis, 324 Australian undergraduates): social cognition constructs (risk perception, subjective norms, attitudes) directly predict intention; drug and health literacy act indirectly (positive); race indirect negative effect. `2025_Piatkowski_Psychological and socio-structural determinants of intentions...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Pocuca 2026 (XS, 833 residential college students, Queensland and Victoria): 68% aware of DCS and over two thirds positive, but unsure whether they would feel comfortable going or whether peers would use it; 12% recently used a commonly tested drug. `2026_Pocuca_Australian University Residential College Students' Awareness...pdf` p1 abstract, p4.
- Valente 2024 (QUAL, 22 Boom Festival DCS users): checking framed as responsibility and a "culture of care" among partygoers; reliance on friends and trusted suppliers. `2024_Valente_Responsibility, Care and Trust...pdf` p1 abstract.

## 6. venue_nightlife

### 6.1 On-site festival drug checking availability
- Moves: ATT up for festival-goers, ADV down (discard/dose intentions), trust shifts toward health services.
- Olsen 2022 (EVAL mixed methods, Australian festival pilot): 170 substances tested for more than 230 patrons, mean age 21; 88% of patrons had an accurate idea of what their drugs contained and 12% (17) had drugs other than expected (corrected against the paper); improved self-reported knowledge, trust in health providers, and use intentions; 9 patrons intended to discard drugs in the amnesty bin and 2 elsewhere; interviewees trusted friends and dealers less afterwards. `2022_Olsen_Music festival drug checking...pdf` p1 abstract, p9 to p10.
- Valente 2022 (XS, Boom Festival 2018, 343 testers vs 115 non-testers): testers planned use and set limits more often (both predicted using DCS); non-testers used most drugs slightly more; DCS reaches mainly highly educated people already using protective strategies. `2022_Valente_Drug use patterns, harm reduction strategies and use of drug checking services in boom festival patrons.pdf` p1 abstract.
- Cruz 2023 (MON/XS, outdoor electronic festival near Mexico City, 40 participants, 51 samples): free, voluntary, confidential two-section station; 92.5% male; most samples had expected drug plus adulterants. `2023_Cruz_First drug-checking study at an electronic festival...pdf` p1 abstract (duplicate preprint `2023_Cruz_Tittle...pdf`).
- Piercey 2025 (XS, Colorado, n = 227): 75.3% ever used reagent kits, 66.5% FTS; "always" or "most of the time" testing 54.4% (RTK) and 59.4% (FTS); 44.4% had a sample test differently than expected. `2025_Piercey_Use of reagent test kits...pdf` p1 abstract, p6.
- Beal 2025 (RCT, small, 50 festival participants): a 10 mg micro-scoop raised interpretation accuracy to 100% vs 84% and clarity ratings 91% vs 73%. `2025_Beal_Enhancing Drug Checking with Human-Centered Design...pdf` p1 abstract.
- MJ 2022 (see 1.7): on-site festival lab revealed substitution invisible to users.
- Usman 2026 (MON, wastewater plus Canberra drug checking, New Year 2024 to 2025): NPS in 15% to 20% of submitted samples; ketamine consumption 2.8 to 3 fold higher on New Year's days at regional sites. `2026_Usman_Understanding Consumption of Illicit Drugs...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 6.2 Licensed venue regulation (alcohol trading hours, lockouts, multicomponent programmes)
- Moves: ADV (violence, alcohol harms) down; little evidence for illicit drugs.
- Eassey 2024 (SR, 48,303 screened, 100 included, 2010 to 2021, PROSPERO): 86 studies on alcohol only, 7 on illicit drugs, 7 both; 88 in licensed entertainment settings; 28 evaluated laws/licensing; multicomponent interventions most common (41); 3 am lockout studies and multicomponent Sydney/Newcastle programmes found significant assault reductions. `2024_Eassey_A systematic review of interventions that impact alcohol and other drug-related harms in licensed entertainment...pdf` p1 abstract, p6 to p10.
- Caveat: the evidence base for illicit drug interventions in venues is thin (14 of 100 studies).

### 6.3 Mobile vs stationary nightlife checking and turnaround
- Moves: ATT up for mobile/quick result services among party-goers.
- Karden 2025: Vienna's mobile service targets music events and returns results within an hour; stationary services require waiting several days, which excludes those unwilling to wait. `2025_Karden...pdf` p2.

### 6.4 Queer, chemsex and kink scenes as settings
- Moves: DEM present but services under-reach these groups unless tailored.
- Coulaud 2024 (QUAL): SGM organisations highly interested; pilot tried multiple outreach configurations to test whether time or location affected access. `2024_Coulaud...pdf` p8.
- García 2023 (MON descriptive, Energy Control Chem-Safe, Spain): chemsex users mostly middle-aged men; cathinones show high substitution, methamphetamine adulteration by addition. `2023_García_Chemsex from Drug Checking Services...pdf` p1 abstract.
- McCormack 2025 (MIXED, London queer creatives): COVID closures and commodification weakened queer nightlife viability, with intersectional inequalities. `2025_McCormack_Building Back Queerer...pdf` p1 abstract.
- McCormack 2022 (XS, 966 English festival respondents, 64 engaged in kink): describes drug and sex practices; no service outcome. `2022_McCormack_Kink in an English Field...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Willeman 2023 (MON, 33 3-MMC users via community organisation AIDES, France, 2021): 86% requested testing; purity 21% to 98%; 15.2% injected. `2023_Willeman_NPAideS...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 6.5 Festival safety and gender-based violence
- Bows 2023 (XS/QUAL, UK festivals): perceptions of safety and gendered victimisation at festivals; no drug checking outcome. `2023_Bows_Perceptions of safety and experiences of gender-based violence...pdf` p1. Skimmed only.

## 7. drug_market_supply

### 7.1 Prevalence of potent adulterants in the opioid supply (fentanyl, xylazine, benzodiazepines, nitazenes)
- Moves: ADV up, DEM up (people want to know), unexpected exposure common.
- Gomez 2025 (MON, NYC SSPs, Nov 2021 to Dec 2024, N = 1027 opioid-containing lab results): 43.7% also contained xylazine; prevalence rose from 10.7% (2021) to 53.7% (2024), trend chi-square 45.229, p < 0.001. `2025_Gomez...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Scarfone 2022 (MON, Toronto multi-site DCS, first 6 months Oct 2019 to Apr 2020, 555 samples): 49% contained high-potency opioids (87% of which also stimulants); benzodiazepine-type in 21%; negative effects (overdose, adverse events, extreme sedation) reported for 11% (59) of samples. `2022_Scarfone_Diverse psychotropic substances...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Tobias 2026b (XS, BC community DCS users, Mar 2020 to Jul 2024, n = 447): 38.9% had unexpected active drugs detected, most commonly benzodiazepines in expected opioids. `2026_Tobias_Risk reduction behaviours following the detection of unexpected drugs...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Swartz 2022/2023 (XS, 118 opioid street users, two Chicago SSPs, 2021 to 2022, LC-MS): 92.1% believed they recently used fentanyl; 38.0% preferred fentanyl, 56.1% did not; participants poor at identifying cuts such as diphenhydramine. `2023_Swartz_Current attitudes toward drug checking services...pdf` p1 abstract (same as `2022_Swartz_...pdf`).
- Park 2024 (cohort preprint, 125 PWUD, Rhode Island, Feb to Aug 2023): 99% wanted to avoid xylazine; 51% knew what it was; only 9% submitted a sample they thought had xylazine, but it was detected in 14% (FTIR) and 21% (lab) of samples, mostly believed to be fentanyl (77%). `2024_Park_Xylazine Awareness, Desire, Use and Exposure...pdf` p2 abstract.
- Summers 2026 (see 2.7): xylazine suspected 9.1%, found 32.7%.
- Friedman 2023 (MIXED, 40 pharmacies in 4 northern Mexico cities): controlled substances obtainable without prescription at 68.3% of pharmacies; counterfeit pills with fentanyl, heroin or methamphetamine found. `2023_Friedman_Fentanyl, Heroin, and Methamphetamine-Based Counterfeit Pills...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Friedman 2022 (ethnography plus drug checking, Tijuana): fentanyl arrival on the US-Mexico border amid surveillance gaps. `2022_Friedman_The introduction of fentanyl on the US-Mexico border...pdf` p1 abstract. Skimmed.
- Gonzalez-Nieto 2025 (MON, BC): the unregulated opioid supply is "not just fentanyl" (analogues, benzodiazepines). `2025_Gonzalez-Nieto_Not just fentanyl...pdf` p2. Skimmed.
- Sisco 2026 (MON preprint, NIST RaDAR, Jan 2024 to Jan 2026): fentanyl samples increasingly contain local anaesthetics, alpha-2 agonists, industrial chemicals, with East/West US differences. `2026_Sisco_Trends in Fentanyl Adulteration...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Man 2026 (MON, 10,749 nitazene price observations across 29 cryptomarkets, Oct 2021 to Sep 2024, plus 10 surface web shops): isotonitazene median USD 80 per gram; strong quantity discounts; surface web cheaper; low prices may widen access. `2026_Man_Price of Nitazenes...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Curtis 2026, Algar 2024 / McLeod 2023 (MON/LAB, CanTEST Canberra): rapid identification of novel nitazene isotocyanozene and three unexpected NPS show early-warning value of fixed-site DCS. `2026_Curtis_...pdf`, `2024_Algar_...pdf`, `2023_McLeod_...pdf` p1 abstracts.

### 7.2 Substitution and adulteration in stimulant, psychedelic, NPS and online markets
- Moves: ADV up; DEM up among people who expect a specific product.
- Barratt 2024 (see 1.1): 21% of cryptomarket samples lacked the advertised drug; 2C-B, alprazolam, ketamine most substituted; only 4 of cocaine samples pure. `2024_Barratt...pdf` p1 abstract (earlier preprint `2023_Barratt_...pdf`).
- MJ 2022 (see 1.7).
- Grifell 2022 (MON online collaborative, 92 analysed NPS samples): 40 different NPS, 13 with no prior literature reports. `2022_Grifell_Identifying new psychoactive substances (NPS)...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Liddle 2025 (MON, Argentina ToxiBot): 1806 of 1890 Marquis results (95.6%) consistent with MDxx family; self-selected; 26,916 web users Aug 2024 to Jul 2026. `2025_Liddle...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Kaskela 2025: amphetamine detected in 85% of samples submitted as amphetamine; atypical substances (methomyl, midazolam, buprenorphine) also seen. `2025_Kaskela...pdf` p5.
- Monti 2023 (MON, Basel DIBS pilot mid-2019 to mid-2022, 744 samples): anonymous, mandatory questionnaire and counselling, results after three days. `2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf` p1.

### 7.3 Trust in the dealer or source of supply
- Moves: DEM down (people see no need), UNMET up (hidden risk).
- Karden 2025: 68% of non-users cited high trust in source and 64% confidence in quality. `2025_Karden...pdf` p1 abstract, p6.
- van 2023 (QUAL, 33 people who buy and sell via social media, New Zealand): established social supply networks reduce perceived risk of adulteration and victimisation; some resell darknet purchases to friends. `2023_van_Social Supply and the Potential for Harm Reduction in Social Media Drug Markets.pdf` p1 abstract.
- Swartz 2022/2023: 35.4% thought there was "no point" testing and 25.2% that DCS was "too much trouble". `2023_Swartz_Current attitudes...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Olsen 2022: trust in dealers fell after testing (see 6.1).
- Reed 2024: informal risk strategies include waiting to see effects on others and buying from the same person. `2024_Reed_Recommendations...pdf` p5.

### 7.4 People who sell drugs using drug checking (supply-chain intervention)
- Moves: ADV down at market level (fewer bad batches), DEM up among sellers.
- Gonzalez-Nieto 2026 (MON retrospective, Victoria BC, Jan 2021 to Dec 2024): 2332 sellers = 16.2% of service users, 5528 samples = 21.6% of all samples; 2.4 samples per visit vs 1.7 for non-sellers; opioids 69.2% of seller submissions vs 35.1% non-sellers (chi-square 2055.57, p < 0.001); safety of self/others the top reason (84.3% sellers vs 76.2% non-sellers). `2026_Gonzalez-Nieto_Drug sellers' use of a drug checking service...pdf` p1 abstract, p4.
- Wallace 2022b: sellers want quality results, written reports, discrete locations without arrest risk, peers with drug knowledge and word of mouth. `2022_Wallace_Implementing drug checking as an illicit drug market intervention...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Pocuca 2025, Puljevic 2025: staff and potential users expect sellers to use checking and the market to respond. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p1 abstract; `2025_Puljević...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Moran 2024: drug checking as informal community-level market regulation filling a regulatory vacuum. `2024_Moran...pdf` p1 abstract.

## 8. service_design_access

### 8.1 Anonymity and confidentiality guaranteed
- Moves: ATT up; absence moves ATT down.
- Pocuca 2025 (QUAL, 23 staff): confidentiality and anonymity concerns are barriers; confidentiality is the first paramount factor. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p1 abstract, p5.
- Puljevic 2025 (QUAL, 57): confidentiality concerns among main barriers. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Grace 2023a: "Prioritizing the anonymity of our participants, 100%"; no identifiable data collected. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment...pdf` p4.
- Hutchison 2025, Davis 2022 (see 5.3).
- Moon 2024: anonymous sample IDs for dashboards. `2024_Moon...pdf` p5.
- Verdenik 2026: anonymous sample collection since 2006 through the early warning system. `2026_Verdenik...pdf` p3.
- Magnolini 2025: anonymous, free AAS checking achieved NPS 97 and CSAT 93%. `2025_Magnolini...pdf` p1 abstract.

### 8.2 Wait time / turnaround time for results
- Moves: ATT down and result collection down as wait rises; effect strongest for dependent users and pre-consumption checking.
- Grace 2023b (ScR): wait time frequently cited as barrier; for people entering withdrawal a 3 to 6 minute wait may be too long; at one Canadian supervised injection site 43% were willing to use DC and 68% of those would wait up to 10 minutes; another study proposed a 30 minute maximum; an Australian study found 80% willing to wait one hour and 61% one week for more reliable results. `2023_Grace_Contextual factors...pdf` p3.
- Kaskela 2025 (EVAL, Finland): results collected for only 208 of 503 samples (41%); staff attributed low return mainly to long waiting time (monthly lab analysis). `2025_Kaskela...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Puljevic 2025: more than about an hour seen by some as not worth it. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p5.
- Karden 2025: mobile result in an hour vs stationary several days (see 6.3).
- Verdenik 2026: results in 4 to 7 days by phone, email or in person. `2026_Verdenik...pdf` p3.
- Falzon 2023: speed of testing a design consideration. `2023_Falzon_Planning...pdf` p2.

### 8.3 Peer staff / staff with lived experience
- Moves: ATT up (trust, reduced perceived stigma); CAP sensitive to legal risk for staff with records.
- Grace 2023a: lived experience frequently identified as crucial for trust and rapport. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment...pdf` p1 abstract, p4.
- Pocuca 2025: peer workers reduce perceived stigma and increase trust; having to interact with a health professional rather than a peer may be a barrier. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p5.
- Puljevic 2025: preference for peer-led services "at all levels". `2025_Puljević...pdf` p6.
- Davis 2022, Wallace 2022a/b: employ peers from diverse backgrounds; pair peers with skilled technicians. `2022_Davis...pdf` p1; `2022_Wallace_...pdf` p1.
- Carroll 2022: staff with lived experience may have criminal records, increasing their exposure to enforcement. `2022_Carroll...pdf` p4.

### 8.4 Location: co-location with trusted low-threshold services vs separate site
- Moves: ATT up when co-located in trusted settings; but co-location with injecting services may deter occasional users.
- Falzon 2023: implement in low-threshold, trusted services with harm reduction ethos. `2023_Falzon_Planning...pdf` p2.
- Pocuca 2025: co-location aids dependent users (anonymity by ambiguity) but NSP stigma may deter infrequent users. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p5.
- Puljevic 2025: a separate location people would not otherwise visit is a barrier. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p5.
- Sutherland 2025: preferred via supervised injecting facility or NSP (93% want DCS). `2025_Sutherland...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Grace 2023a: "nodes" maximising public health impact; brick-and-mortar requirement could deter clients. `2023_Grace_A qualitative assessment...pdf` p4 to p5.
- Summers 2026: on-site in housing preferred. `2026_Summers...pdf` p8.

### 8.5 Mobile / outreach modality and flexible times
- Moves: ATT up for hard-to-reach and event populations; CAP lower per site.
- Bird 2026: 50% of LA programmes mobile vs 7% US; US more stationary (46% vs 0%) and on-site (89% vs 60%). `2026_Bird...pdf` p7.
- Coulaud 2024: multiple outreach configurations to test effect of time and location. `2024_Coulaud...pdf` p8.
- Ondocsin 2023: mobile services used to avoid legal scrutiny of a fixed site. `2023_Ondocsin...pdf` p7.
- Gozdzialski 2025: remote kiosk sites extended reach (see 3.5).

### 8.6 Cost to client (free vs charged; giving up part of the drug)
- Moves: ATT down with cost; free supplies raise uptake.
- Puljevic 2025: cost could be "a massive barrier"; dependent users reluctant to give up a sample. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p5 to p6.
- Sutherland 2025: 95% (54/57) would keep using FTS if free; 97% would recommend to peers. `2025_Sutherland...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Piercey 2025: cost and sourcing of supplies a barrier at festivals. `2025_Piercey_Use of reagent test kits...pdf` discussion.
- Kirk 2025: free distribution plus campaigns raised demand (see 3.3).
- Grace 2023b: in one US study 94% willing to provide a sample, varying by amount (residue 35%, pinhead to bump 36%, whatever it takes 24%). `2023_Grace_Contextual factors...pdf` p3.

### 8.7 Result quality, quantification and technology accuracy (trust)
- Moves: DEM and repeat ATT up with quantitative, comprehensive, reliable results; false negatives raise ADV and erode trust.
- Swartz 2022/2023: users most value quantitative concentrations and identification of all constituents. `2023_Swartz_Current attitudes...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Wallace 2022b: quality results and written reports increase relative advantage. `2022_Wallace_Implementing...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Moran 2024: clients worry about accuracy and about hard choices after unexpected results. `2024_Moran...pdf` p10.
- Molina 2025 (LAB/field, Los Angeles, 333 fentanyl-positive samples with both XTS and DART-MS): XTS sensitivity 49.3% (34/69), specificity 89.0%; 78% of xylazine-positive samples had <1% xylazine by weight. `2025_Molina_Assessing the Real-World Performance of Xylazine Test Strips...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Reed 2025 (simulation, 40 PWUD, 2023): although 80% reported prior FTS training, none used FTS as recommended (most under-diluted, likely false positives); correct interpretation 45% for positive, 55% for clear negative, 30% for negative with faint line. `2025_Reed_Urgent Need for Pairing Training and Education...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Piercey 2025b (content analysis, 16 online FTS instruction sets, May 2024): considerable variability, especially testing methods; could lead to distrust and disengagement. `2025_Piercey_Highlighting variability in fentanyl test strip instructions...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Beal 2025 (see 6.1).
- Halifax 2024, Wu 2024, Hayes 2023, De 2024, Marland 2025, Al-Juhani 2025 (LAB): lot-to-lot variability, blind spots and false positives in FTS and nitazene strips (one-line notes in the lab list below).

### 8.8 Take-home self-testing kits (FTS, reagents)
- Moves: ATT (to testing) up, ADV down modestly via dose reduction; reach beyond fixed sites.
- Sutherland 2025 (cohort, 78 heroin users, Sydney, 10 FTS each, 4-week follow-up n = 72): 81% used at least one (median 6); among 25 with positive results, 48% used less or started smaller, 60% shared information. `2025_Sutherland...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Tilhou 2022 (MIXED, Wisconsin): 36.5% of FTS users use them most of the time or more; 80.6% get positives at least half the time; testing rates 99.1% heroin, 56.8% fentanyl, 42.2% methamphetamine, 55.7% cocaine/crack among regular users; supported by supplier information, transport, diverse distribution points, staff recommendations, a safe or private place to use. `2022_Tilhou...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Tilhou 2023 (XS, n = 341): FTS users reported more safer (p = 0.001) and more riskier (p = 0.018) behaviours than non-users. `2023_Tilhou_Differences in drug use behaviors...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Naidu 2026 (pooled XS, Rhode Island 2021 to 2023, n = 498): only 31.7% used FTS in past 30 days despite distribution; associated with age, race/ethnicity, witnessing overdose, carrying naloxone, recent fentanyl/heroin use (unadjusted). `2026_Naidu_Exploratory Analysis...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Reyes 2025 (XS, 400 street-recruited users, Puerto Rico SSPs, Mar to Jun 2024): 76.5% willing to use FTS (see 9.2 for predictors). `2025_Reyes_Willingness to use fentanyl test strips...pdf` p1 abstract (duplicate preprint present).
- Swartz 2023b (feasibility, 12 Chicago SSP clients, 21 days): daily reporting on 63.5% of person-days (160/252). `2023_Swartz_Feasibility study of using mobile phone-based experience sampling...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Reed 2022 (QUAL, 29): motivations driven by knowledge, access, neighbourhood and market trends; few logistical barriers. `2022_Reed_You cant go wrong...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Fadanelli 2026, Kannam 2024, Rife-Pennington 2026 (see 3.2, 4.3).
- Short 2024 (RCT protocol, 2400 participants, 28 Ohio counties, county-level randomisation): no results yet. `2024_Short_Evaluating fentanyl test strips...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Klaire 2022 (take-home drug checking BC): only a CrimRxiv cover page in the PDF, no content.

### 8.9 Training and education paired with distribution
- Moves: effectiveness of ATT translating to correct use up; ADV down.
- Reed 2025 (see 8.7), Rife-Pennington 2026 (knowledge d up to 1.21), Larson 2022/2025 (prioritised trainings), Marley 2022/2025 (88.3% of pharmacists want training).

### 8.10 Drug alerts and public warnings
- Moves: ADV down via intention not to use; DEM for checking up.
- Keygan 2025 (randomised online vignette, Australians who used MDMA pills, alert n = 441 vs control n = 184): 45.4% of alert groups intended not to use vs 20.7% of control; intention not to use vs smaller dose aRRR 3.28 (95% CI 2.13 to 5.07), vs same/higher dose aRRR 2.62 (1.31 to 5.22); wording variations made no significant difference. `2025_Keygan_Risk communication about high-dose MDMA...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Verdenik 2026: average 24 public health warnings per year from DCS data. `2026_Verdenik...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Kaskela 2025: a sample prompted by a police warning about grey owl tablets turned out to be MDMA only, consistent with forensic lab. `2025_Kaskela...pdf` p5.
- Caveat: Keygan measures hypothetical intention only.

### 8.11 Drug checking as entry point to other services (hidden populations)
- Moves: ATT to wider harm reduction and treatment up; UNMET down.
- Verdenik 2026: 82.9% had no prior contact with drug services. `2026_Verdenik...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Kaskela 2025: over two thirds of submitting visitors had never used substance use services. `2025_Kaskela...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Berry 2025: +74% SSP encounters. `2025_Berry...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Larson 2022: 1.44 referrals or services per kit. `2022_Larson...pdf` p2.
- Karden 2025: DC-experienced respondents more often in treatment in past 12 months (14.3% vs 6.5%); cross-sectional, no causality. `2025_Karden...pdf` p4.
- Calle 2022 (OP letter, on Dutch DCS data): 76.1% of Dutch fixed-site DCS clients were university educated and people with migration background under-represented, so claims of reaching "difficult-to-reach" groups are not supported. `2022_Calle_How (not) to prove...pdf` p6.

### 8.12 Counselling and brief interventions delivered with results
- Moves: knowledge up, ADV down (intention).
- Olsen 2022, Verdenik 2026 (73.7% new information), Monti 2023 (mandatory counselling in Basel). See 6.1, 3.1, 7.2.

### 8.13 Service tailored to specific substances or groups (cannabis, AAS, SGM, students)
- Moves: DEM met for groups ignored by generic services.
- Bastien 2025 (cannabis, 73.4% willing), Magnolini 2025 (AAS), Coulaud 2024 (SGM men), Pocuca 2026 (students). See above.
- Airth 2024 (OP comment): calls for responsible collection and reporting of demographic data to judge equity of DCS access. `2024_Airth_Comment on Rose et al...pdf` p1.

## 9. individual

### 9.1 Receiving an unexpected result -> risk reduction behaviour
- Moves: ADV down (dose reduction, disposal, not using); strongest when the expected drug is absent.
- Tobias 2026a ChecKnow (XS baseline of French e-cohort, n = 1363, 480 (35.2%) ever used DCS): vs expected-only results, risk-reducing behaviour prevalence higher for diluted (aPR 2.78, 95% CI 0.84 to 9.18, ns), adulterated (aPR 3.34, 1.52 to 7.34) and expected drug absent (aPR 5.48, 2.61 to 11.50). `2026_Tobias_Discordant Drug Checking Results and Risk Reduction Behaviours...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Tobias 2026b (XS, BC, n = 447): unexpected active drug detected associated with risk reduction, aOR 2.24 (95% CI 1.30 to 3.87); most common actions: disposal without use (n = 24), smaller dose (n = 11). `2026_Tobias_Risk reduction behaviours...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Verdenik 2024 (prospective two-wave survey, Slovenian integrated DCS, 364 clients): intention to avoid use when sample not as expected in 65.5% of cases; strong correlation between sample health-risk level and attitudes to use; follow-up at 10 to 14 days. `2024_Verdenik_'I will not use it because the results are bad'...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Verdenik 2026: 23% intended not to use after an unexpected result. `2026_Verdenik...pdf` p10.
- Sutherland 2025: 48% of positive FTS results led to using less. Kannam 2024: 62.1% would not use or dispose. Fadanelli 2026: 10 of 11 positives changed behaviour.
- Maghsoudi 2022 (SR, 90 studies, 72.2% Europe): evidence that DCS influences behavioural intention, smaller subset on enacted behaviour; overdose outcomes not yet shown. `2022_Maghsoudi_The Implementation of Drug Checking Services...pdf` p1 to p2 abstract.
- Tilhou 2023: FTS users also report more risky behaviours (confounding by risk level). `2023_Tilhou...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Caveats: self-report, cross-sectional, response bias (Verdenik 2026 follow-up response 13.5%, n = 153; second follow-up n = 32).

### 9.2 Frequency of use and dependence
- Moves: frequent opioid users use DCS more often (ATT up); severe dependence lowers willingness and ability to wait or give a sample (ATT down).
- Airth 2026 (XS, 220 BC DCS users, 2021 to 2023): suspected unintentional benzodiazepine use associated with DCS frequency (OR 2.11, 1.22 to 3.69) but not after adjustment (aOR 1.49, 0.75 to 2.99); frequent unregulated opioid use best explained frequency; no gender modification; 50% used more than once. `2026_Airth_Unintentional benzodiazepine use...pdf` p1 abstract, p4.
- Reyes 2025 (XS, n = 400, Puerto Rico): engagement in treatment OR 3.09 (1.81 to 5.26); severe SUD OR 0.36 (0.18 to 0.73). `2025_Reyes...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Puljevic 2025: people with dependence less motivated and face more barriers. `2025_Puljević...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Pocuca 2025: staff think infrequent users are most likely to access. `2025_Pocuca...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Grace 2023b: people entering withdrawal unlikely to wait. `2023_Grace_Contextual factors...pdf` p3.

### 9.3 Education and socioeconomic position
- Moves: higher education -> DEM and ATT up; implies inequity (UNMET concentrated in less educated).
- Valente 2022, Calle 2022 (76.1% university educated), Bastien 2025 (tertiary aOR 2.14), Caluzzi 2023 (support 65% with degree), Karden 2025 (secondary vs primary education OR 1.73; part-time OR 2.4, unemployed OR 2.79 vs students). `2025_Karden...pdf` p4 to p5.

### 9.4 Gender
- Moves: mixed; women sometimes less willing or under-represented.
- Bastien 2025 (women aOR 0.54), Verdenik 2026 (81% of users men), Akrigg 2026 (men more lab testing 13.0% vs 3.7%), Airth 2026 (no modification), Karden 2025 (no gender effect), Silcox 2026 (community submitters 50.1% male vs police 78.6%).

### 9.5 Risk perception, perceived benefits, comfort, drug literacy
- Moves: DEM up.
- Hohmann 2026 (XS, n = 206): perceived susceptibility (beta 0.442), benefits (beta 0.250) and comfort (beta 0.453) predicted FTS intention, R2 = 0.417. `2026_Hohmann...pdf` p1 abstract.
- Piatkowski 2025a (see 5.5).

### 9.6 Fentanyl preference or seeking
- Moves: DEM for avoidance-type checking down; checking used to confirm desired drug.
- Swartz 2022/2023 (38.0% prefer fentanyl), Tilhou 2022 (seeking fentanyl associated with FTS use), Kannam 2024 (one confirmed fentanyl as preference), Murphy 2025 (police fear FTS used to seek fentanyl).

### 9.7 Planning and protective behaviour repertoire
- Moves: those already careful attend more (selection effect).
- Valente 2022 (testers plan and set limits more), Reed 2024 (multiple informal strategies), Piercey 2025.

### 9.8 Overdose experience, naloxone possession
- Moves: DEM up.
- Naidu 2026 (witnessing overdose and carrying naloxone associated with FTS use), Swartz 2022/2023 (mean 4.4 lifetime and 1.1 past-year overdoses), Karden 2025 discussion.

### 9.9 Race and ethnicity
- Moves: under-reach of some groups.
- Tilhou 2022 (FTS users more often non-Hispanic white), Naidu 2026, Hohmann 2026 (non-White higher perceived susceptibility, 2.06 vs 1.91), Piatkowski 2025a (race negative indirect effect), Silcox 2026 (Black 15.8% PD vs 7.8% CP).
