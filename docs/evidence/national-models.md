# National drug checking models as products of their legal and cultural environment

Purpose: evidence-backed presets for the harm-reduction city sandbox (drop-in centre, mobile outreach camper, club harm-reduction / drug-checking stand) with top-to-bottom context factors.

## Method and caveats

- Library (read only): `/Volumes/2TB/Data/Cloud/MEGA-Sync/MEGAsync/Chemical Safety/Wiedza/HR Drug Checking/`. Poland law data come from sibling folders `Wiedza/narkopolityka/` and `Wiedza/_polskie/` (via factors-C, with the art. 62a and detection cells re-checked in the xlsx).
- Indexes used: factors-B.md, factors-C.md, and the slice-A drafts partA_aa to partA_ad.md (factors-A.md was not present). Every value below was re-checked against the paper text, except where marked as secondary.
- Page numbers are PDF page indices of the PDFKit text extraction (page 1 = first PDF page, which can be a cover page in preprints). The Koning manuscript is a .docx without pages, so sections are cited. Two files missing from the earlier extraction (Brunt 2011, Pirona 2016) were extracted for this pass.
- Typographic dashes and quotes inside some library filenames are written here as plain ASCII; long filenames are shortened with '...'.
- Value scales: possession_law (criminal / depenalised or diverted / decriminalised / regulated legal); checking_legal_status (prohibited / grey or tolerated / licensed or explicitly legal / state-run); funding (none or volunteer / project or short-term / stable national); policing_relation (hostile or visible deterrence / neutral / cooperative or formal agreement); turnaround (on-site minutes / days / weeks); geographic coverage (single city / several cities / national); climate (supportive / mixed / opposed).
- 'no evidence in library' means the library holds no usable statement; nothing was filled from memory. Many legal facts are dated (2001 EMCDDA, 2004 Hunt) and are flagged with their year.
- Corrections to earlier notes: Olsen 2022 reports that 88% of patrons had a generally accurate perception of contents and 12% (n = 17) had drugs different from expectation (factors-B said 88% of results matched). Verdenik 2026 reports 4,338 visits in its table and about 2,759 estimated unique users, but its text also calls 2,759 'visits'. `Brunt_2011b...pdf` is correctly the DIMS review; the file `Understanding reasons for drug use...` is Boys 2001, unrelated. `2025_Airth_...pdf` actually contains the UNODC World Drug Report 2024 key findings.

## Netherlands

Confidence: **high**. Primary DIMS sources (Brunt 2011, Vogels 2009, Koning AAM, EMCDDA 2001) agree; post-2011 funding figures absent.

- **possession_law**: depenalised or diverted (cannabis: tolerated retail under formal prohibition). Producing, possessing and selling drugs is an offence but using them is not; small cannabis quantities fall under an expediency principle and coffeeshop retail is de facto tolerance, not a legal market. [Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p1; 2004_NEIL_HUNT_harm reduction review.pdf p27, p28 to p29]
- **checking_legal_status**: licensed or explicitly legal (state-coordinated national network). Pill testing is part of official drug policy approved by Parliament, with special agreements with the Ministry of Justice and prosecutors; samples from obvious dealers or producers are refused. The Ministry of Health made testing possible since the 1990s. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p10, p18; Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p1]
- **funding**: stable national. Government-funded since 1992; in 2000 about EUR 500,000 per year for national coordination plus about EUR 7,000 per participating organisation. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p18; 2019_Measham_Drug safety testing, disposals and dealing in an English field...pdf p3]
- **policing_relation**: cooperative or formal agreement. Formal agreements with Justice and prosecutors; police forwarded seized samples to the monitoring system for comparison (2004 to 2006). [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p10; Vogels et al 2009.pdf p3, p7]
- **service_model**: fixed site; lab send-away; festival or nightlife (historical, 1992 to 1998, restart planned 2000/2001). Anonymous submission during weekly consultation hours at 31 fixed sites run by addiction-prevention institutions; unrecognised samples go to the national lab via the DIMS Bureau. [RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Introduction section (docx, unpaginated); Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p2; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p10, p18 to p19]
- **anonymity and cost to client**: anonymous; small fee (EUR 3 per stationary analysis in 2000; no later figure in library). Anonymity is described as a main condition of trust; 2000 fact file lists EUR 3 per analysis (EUR 2 planned on-site). [Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p3; RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Introduction section; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p19]
- **turnaround**: on-site minutes for recognised tablets; about one week for lab analysis. Office testing with a weekly updated recognition list (99% reliable) resolves about 30% of tablets immediately; other results are given about a week later by phone. [Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p2; Vogels et al 2009.pdf p2 to p3; RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Introduction section]
- **early warning / public alerts**: yes, core function. Authorities act immediately on dangerous drugs: network alerts, flyers in clubs, regional or national press warnings, and notification of EU early warning systems; atropine and PMA warnings documented. [Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p2, p5, p8; Vogels et al 2009.pdf p8 to p9]
- **geographic coverage**: national. 31 fixed-site services in 2018 (25 test offices across 1993 to 2008; 8 cities in 2000). [RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Introduction section; Vogels et al 2009.pdf p2; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p18]
- **community and political climate**: supportive. Drugs treated primarily as a health issue with the Minister of Health coordinating policy; harm reduction a main feature of policy. [Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p1]
- **client profile**: young, male, highly educated, nightlife-active, experienced users. 2018 survey (n = 1,530, 8 services): 63.2% male, 59.1% in their twenties, mean age 28.5, 83.8% no migration background, 76.1% university educated, 93.0% lifetime MDMA, 0.7% had used none of 20 drugs. 1999: mean age 22.3, 80% male. [RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Results section; 2022_Calle_How (not) to prove that drug checking services facilitate access...pdf p6; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p18]
- **key outcomes**: large-scale market monitoring; warnings linked to disappearance of dangerous tablets (observational). 12,634 samples in 2018; over 100,000 samples in about two decades; 33,006 lab-analysed tablets 1993 to 2008 (about 2,000 per year); about 500 pills per month in 1999. Atropine tablets fell after a 1997 warning and disappeared after 2000; PMA 30 cases 2001, 8 in 2002, none 2003 to 2009 (secondary). NPS share 0.5% (2007) to 11% (2017). [RPJ_Koning_Dutch_drug_checking_system_AAM.docx Introduction section; Brunt_2011b drug  information monitoring system in the netherlands DIMS.pdf p1; Vogels et al 2009.pdf p1, p8 to p9; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p18; 2016_Australia_Pill-Testing-Report.pdf p25; 2021_Hirschfeld_Safer Tripping Serotonergic Psychedelics and Drug Checking...pdf p3]

How the environment shaped the model: A health-led, pragmatic national policy in which use is not an offence let the Ministry of Health build checking into the addiction-prevention infrastructure from 1992, with a national coordinator and lab. Because ecstasy is legally a hard drug, legitimacy rested on justice agreements, a dealer exclusion rule and a monitoring framing, which made the early warning function central. The resulting model is institutional (weekly office hours, lab send-away) and reaches mostly highly educated nightlife users rather than marginalised groups.

## Switzerland

Confidence: **high**. Several primary service papers (Zurich, Basel, Bern); national possession law only in a dated secondary source.

- **possession_law**: depenalised or diverted (dated source); low-THC cannabis under 1% THC regulated legal. Hunt 2004 lists Switzerland under partial prohibition (personal-use activities not punished, commerce illegal), citing a 2002 source; no current statement in library. Low-THC (<1%) cannabis products except hashish are legal. [2004_NEIL_HUNT_harm reduction review.pdf p27; 2022_Monti_Adulteration of low-delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol products with synthetic cannabinoids...pdf p1]
- **checking_legal_status**: licensed or explicitly legal. Drug checking is legally anchored in the Swiss Narcotics Act as a harm-reducing service with no legal consequences for clients; in 1998/99 two legal opinions confirmed legality if linked to prevention messages. [2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p12, p16]
- **funding**: stable (municipal and cantonal). Zurich DIZ is a public service of the city's Department of Social Welfare; Basel DIBS involves the cantonal health department and the Institute of Forensic Medicine; early pilots were publicly subsidised (Pilot E about EUR 71,000). [2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p12, p16]
- **policing_relation**: cooperative or formal agreement. Pilot E had support of public authorities, local police and party organisers; current services state there are no legal consequences for clients. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p12; 2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2]
- **service_model**: fixed site; festival or nightlife; integrated into harm reduction (specialised AAS checking). Zurich Streetwork on-site checking since 2001 (about ten events a year) and stationary DIZ since 2006; Basel stationary DIBS; AAS checking added in Zurich in 2023. [2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group...pdf p1 to p2; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; 2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p1 to p3]
- **anonymity and cost to client**: anonymous and free; questionnaire and counselling mandatory. Clients stay anonymous, testing is cost-free, and completing a questionnaire plus counselling is an obligation ('return service'). [2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2 to p3; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; 2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group...pdf p2]
- **turnaround**: on-site minutes (festival HPLC); days at fixed sites (Basel: 3 days). Austrian and Swiss HPLC field labs return results within about 20 minutes; Basel informs clients after three days. [2016_Butterfield_Drug checking to improve monitoring of new psychoactive substances in Australia.pdf p3; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1]
- **early warning / public alerts**: yes. DIZ incorporates an alert system for substances of concern; Basel issues public warnings; Swiss services feed the European TEDI network, which detected SC-adulterated low-THC cannabis. [2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; Cannabis-adulterated-with-the-synthetic-cannabinoid-rec_2022_International-J.pdf p2]
- **geographic coverage**: several cities. Zurich, Basel, Bern (pilot) and Geneva (MALDI-HRMS lab work) appear in the library; no national count. [2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group...pdf p1; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p16; 2020_Joye_High-Throughput Qualitative and Quantitative Drug Checking by MALDI HRMS.pdf p1]
- **community and political climate**: supportive. Swiss drug policy signed into law aims to reduce harm for people who use substances and society. [2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p2; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p12]
- **client profile**: nightlife polydrug users in their late twenties; mostly men. Zurich 2001 to 2010 (n = 1,376): 21.9% women, mean age 27.8, daily cannabis 27.2% vs 9.3% general population; stationary clients older (30.7 vs 27.0), more female (29.2% vs 19.8%), more tertiary educated (30.8% vs 16.7%). Bern 1999: 18 to 26, 80% male. [2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group...pdf p2 to p4; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p16]
- **key outcomes**: steady volume; detection of specific market risks. Zurich 2001 to 2010: 84 events and 172 stationary days, 7,622 consultations, 2,055 HPLC analyses; polydrug use fell 13.0% 2004 to 2009. Basel mid-2019 to mid-2022: 744 samples (cocaine 25%). Low-THC cannabis 2020 to 2021: 50% of 94 samples had synthetic cannabinoids. AAS pilot: 52 clients, 71 samples, 52% fake. [2011_Hungerbuehler_Drug Checking A prevention measure for a heterogeneous group...pdf p2, p5; 2023_Monti_Drug Checking Glimpse into the Recreational Drug Market in Switzerland.pdf p1; 2022_Monti_Adulteration of low-delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol products with synthetic cannabinoids...pdf p1; 2025_Magnolini_Evaluation of implementing drug checking services for anabolic androgenic steroids in Switzerland...pdf p1]

How the environment shaped the model: Switzerland's pragmatic harm-reduction policy and explicit legal anchoring let cities run checking as an ordinary municipal social or health service, with anonymous, free testing and compulsory counselling. Zurich's large party economy after hospitality-law relaxation drove early on-site work, while the legal low-THC market created a specific synthetic-cannabinoid risk that the services were positioned to detect. The legal clarity also allowed scope extension to new groups such as steroid users.

## Austria

Confidence: **medium**. Good service evidence (Karden 2025, EMCDDA 2001); no library evidence on current possession law or national early warning links.

- **possession_law**: no evidence in library. Library describes checking legality but not possession penalties.
- **checking_legal_status**: licensed or explicitly legal (conditional). Ministry of Justice and Ministry of Social Affairs and Health statements declare testing legal if done by a scientific institution; staff may not touch or hand back substances. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p4, p14]
- **funding**: stable (provincial and municipal public funding). All Austrian services are mainly publicly funded; ChEck iT! had about EUR 145,000 per year in 2000 and is now part of Vienna Addiction Services. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p3, p9; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p14]
- **policing_relation**: cooperative or formal agreement (non-targeting). Police are present at raves but do not concentrate on ChEck iT! visitors and support its preventive aims. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p4; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Report.pdf p36]
- **service_model**: fixed site; festival or nightlife (mobile); pharmacy drop-off (Vienna). All services offer stationary checking at a counselling facility; Vienna adds mobile checking at events and drop-off sites in selected pharmacies. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p2]
- **anonymity and cost to client**: anonymous and free. Publicly funded services are free and anonymous; on-site clients identify results by number on a public board. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p9; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p15]
- **turnaround**: on-site within an hour (mobile); several days (stationary). Vienna's mobile service returns results within an hour; stationary services require waiting several days. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p2]
- **early warning / public alerts**: partial (public posting of results); national early warning link not documented. Results are posted near the tent and published online. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p14 to p15]
- **geographic coverage**: several cities. Four services in five cities (Bludenz, Dornbirn, Graz, Innsbruck, Vienna) in four federal states; only Vienna and Innsbruck in 2021. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p1 to p2, p8]
- **community and political climate**: supportive (regional). Pill testing is part of official Vienna drug policy passed by majority; no national drug policy concept existed in 2001. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p4]
- **client profile**: stimulant and polydrug users; urban; few cannabis-only users. Web survey 2021 (n = 1,113): 20.1% ever used; users more often used stimulants (59.8% vs 26.8%) and less often cannabis only (9.4% vs 30.7%). 1999 on-site: mainly 16 to 19, 70% male. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p1, p6; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p15]
- **key outcomes**: one in five users reached; rural unmet need. Non-users cited trust in source (68%), confidence in quality (64%) and no nearby service (62%); missing availability 82.6% rural vs 37.7% urban. 1999: 5 events, average 250 people and 62 pills per event. Secondary: two thirds of those warned chose not to consume. [2025_Karden_Utilization of drug checking services in Austria a cross-sectional online survey.pdf p1, p8; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p15; 2016_Australia_Pill-Testing-Report.pdf p22; 2018_Groves_Worth the test Pragmatism, pill testing and drug policy in Australia.pdf p8]

How the environment shaped the model: A federal state without a national drug policy concept left checking to the provinces, so Vienna's majority-backed policy produced the first stable, publicly funded service. The legal condition that a scientific institution must analyse and staff must not handle substances shaped the hospital-toxicology partnership and self-scraping HPLC procedure. Concentration in a few cities leaves rural users with high unmet need.

## Belgium

Confidence: **low**. Only a 2001 fact file plus scattered mentions; no recent service evaluation in library.

- **possession_law**: depenalised or diverted (cannabis only, dated); other substances: no evidence in library. Expediency principle: cases with defined small cannabis quantities not investigated or prosecuted. [2004_NEIL_HUNT_harm reduction review.pdf p27]
- **checking_legal_status**: grey or tolerated (research framing). Accepted by the federal health ministry as research; legality of giving results to consumers unclear; no official local acceptance (2001). [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p5, p34; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Summary.pdf p6]
- **funding**: project or short-term. Future activities depended on funding and organisers (2001). [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p34]
- **policing_relation**: hostile or visible deterrence. Local police disapproved and discouraged users by massive physical presence. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p5, p34]
- **service_model**: festival or nightlife; fixed site (Modus Fiesta). Festival tent with Marquis tests and lab GC-MS; Modus Fiesta listed as fixed plus mobile for partygoers. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p34 to p35; 2022_Maghsoudi_The Implementation of Drug Checking Services for People Who Use Drugs A Systematic Review.pdf p20]
- **anonymity and cost to client**: free; anonymity: no evidence in library. Consumers do not pay for analysis. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p35]
- **turnaround**: on-site minutes (reagent); lab results next festival day. Pills sent to the lab were returned the next day of the festival. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p35]
- **early warning / public alerts**: partial. A Belgian Early Warning System on Drugs exists within Sciensano; Modus Vivendi reported NPS findings; direct linkage not documented. [2024_De_Nitazene test strips a laboratory evaluation.pdf p3; 2021_Hirschfeld_Safer Tripping Serotonergic Psychedelics and Drug Checking...pdf p5; 2023_Balcaen_Challenges in Drug Surveillance Strengthening the Analysis of New Psychoactive Substances...pdf p1]
- **geographic coverage**: single region (Brussels and Wallonia); Flemish partners appear only in standards work. Modus Vivendi in Brussels; Flemish organisations listed as NEWIP standards contributors. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p34; 2015_EU_NEWIP_Drug checking_standards.pdf p5]
- **community and political climate**: mixed. Federal research acceptance versus local non-acceptance; Belgium adopted precautionary zero-tolerance NPS measures. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p5; 2017_Chatwin_New drugs, new directions Research priorities for new psychoactive substances...pdf p2]
- **client profile**: no evidence in library. A 2006 Belgian partygoer comparison (n = 486) is cited without profile detail. [2022_Maghsoudi_The Implementation of Drug Checking Services for People Who Use Drugs A Systematic Review.pdf p7]
- **key outcomes**: sparse. Two of 287 on-site samples sold as amphetamine, cocaine or ketamine contained 5-MeO-MiPT or N-Et-2,3-MDPEA; planned volume over 150 pills per event (2001). [2021_Hirschfeld_Safer Tripping Serotonergic Psychedelics and Drug Checking...pdf p5; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p34]

How the environment shaped the model: Split competences (research federal, prevention communal) gave checking a research-only legitimacy without local political cover, while local police actively deterred attendance. The result in 2001 was a festival-based, research-framed service that depended on organisers and short-term money. The library has no later evidence to show how this evolved.

## Spain

Confidence: **medium**. Energy Control well documented across 1999 to 2023; legal status and funding mostly from 2001 files.

- **possession_law**: depenalised or diverted (private possession); punishable in public places (dated). Partial prohibition: possession only punishable if for consumption in public places. [2004_NEIL_HUNT_harm reduction review.pdf p27]
- **checking_legal_status**: grey or tolerated. Not entirely clear whether provisions allow or forbid testing; local and regional authorities and police in favour, federal government accepts; services note the need for support 'despite the legal framework'. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p11, p24; 2023_García_Chemsex from Drug Checking Services descriptive study on methamphetamine, mephedrone...pdf p3]
- **funding**: project or short-term (NGO with public support). Energy Control budget about EUR 50,000 in 2000; most EU project budgets 80 to 100% public; no later funding data. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p24; 2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Summary.pdf p5]
- **policing_relation**: cooperative or formal agreement (local). Barcelona municipality and police are familiar with and support Energy Control. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p11, p24]
- **service_model**: festival or nightlife; fixed site; lab send-away (postal, including international); integrated (chemsex sexual-health service). On-site tents at raves and outside clubs; stationary testing via office and hospital lab; mail-in since 1999; four offices (Andalusia, Balearic Islands, Catalonia, Madrid); chemsex service SIASAS since 2021 with LGBT partners. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p24 to p25; 2023_García_Chemsex from Drug Checking Services descriptive study on methamphetamine, mephedrone...pdf p3, p5; 2016_Caudevilla_Results of an international drug testing service for cryptomarket users.pdf p7]
- **anonymity and cost to client**: free in person; international postal service EUR 50 with no personal data. On-site and stationary analysis free; cryptomarket service charges 50 EUR (Bitcoin or PayPal) and collects no personal data. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p25; 2016_Caudevilla_Results of an international drug testing service for cryptomarket users.pdf p7]
- **turnaround**: on-site minutes (reagents); lab days (no exact figure in library). Marquis tests on site; GC in hospital lab for stationary and postal samples. [2001_EMCDDA_Pill_Testing_Fact_Files.pdf p25]
- **early warning / public alerts**: yes. Energy Control notified 50 (2015) and 82 (2016) new NPS to the Spanish Early Warning System; dangerous results announced to the whole audience. ## Gaps that matter for the sandbox

- No library evidence on current possession law for Austria, Slovenia, Finland or New Zealand, and only dated (2004) statements for Spain, Switzerland, Belgium and Germany.
- No mobile-camper style outreach evaluation outside Latin America (50% of programmes mobile) and Vienna's mobile event service; the outreach camper preset should borrow turnaround and staffing from festival models.
- No attendance figures linked to a change in legal regime within one country; legal and policing effects on attendance are mostly stated intentions (for example 94% would not use a service if arrest were possible).
- No Polish drug checking service evidence at all.
